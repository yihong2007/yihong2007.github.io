Mythologies

A myth is a story about the remote past which is considered true within the society in which it is told.
Creation myths take place long ago, during the first existing period of time: the primordial age.
Legend refers to the collected myths of a group of people — their body of stories which they tell to explain nature, history, and customs.
It can also refer to the study of such myths. Myths are often associated with religion or spirituality and can be very important to those who believe in them.

Greek Mythology

Origin:

Greek mythology came from an ancient oral tradition blending Proto-Indo-European roots, Minoan-Mycenaean cultures, and Near Eastern influences.

Introduction:

Greek mythology is the body of myths originally told by the ancient Greeks, and a genre of ancient Greek folklore, today absorbed alongside Roman mythology into the broader designation of classical mythology. These stories concern the origin and nature of the world, the lives of deities, and heroes and the significance of the ancient Greeks' cult and ritual practices. Modern scholars study the myths to shed light on the religious and political institutions of ancient Greece, and to better understand the nature of mythmaking itself.

Historical Events:

Creation and Primordial Era:

From Chaos emerged Gaia (Earth) and Uranus (Sky). Uranus imprisoned his children, leading Gaia to help her youngest son, Cronus, overthrow his father.

The Titanomachy:

A ten-year divine war where Zeus and his Olympian siblings fought the older Titan gods for control of the cosmos. Zeus won, casting the Titans into Tartarus.The Golden and Heroic Ages: Humanity went through multiple cycles, from a peaceful Golden Age to the Heroic Age, which featured legendary adventures like Heracles' 12 labors and Jason's quest for the Golden Fleece.

The Trojan War:

A massive ten-year siege of Troy by a united Greek army led by King Agamemnon. The conflict was triggered by Paris of Troy taking Helen of Sparta and ended with the clever stratagem of the Trojan Horse.

Norse Mythology

Origin:

Norse mythology originated in Scandinavia—the modern-day countries of Norway, Sweden, and Denmark — with deep roots also extending to Iceland.

Introduction

Norse, Nordic, or Scandinavian mythology, is the body of myths belonging to the North Germanic peoples, stemming from Old Norse religion and continuing after the Christianization of Scandinavia as the Nordic folklore of the modern period. The northernmost extension of Germanic mythology and stemming from Proto-Germanic folklore, Norse mythology consists of tales of various deities, beings, and heroes derived from numerous sources from both before and after the pagan period, including medieval manuscripts, archaeological representations, and folk tradition. The source texts mention numerous gods such as the thunder-god Thor, the raven-flanked god Odin, the goddess Freyja, and numerous other deities.

Historical Events:

Æsir–Vanir War:

Æsir–Vanir War was a conflict between two groups of deities that ultimately resulted in the unification of the Æsir and the Vanir into a single pantheon. The war is an important event in Norse mythology, and the implications for the potential historicity surrounding accounts of the war are a matter of scholarly debate and discourse.

Fimbulwinter:

Fimbulwinter is the harsh winter that precedes the end of the world. Fimbulwinter is three successive winters, when snow comes in from all directions, without any intervening summer. Innumerable wars follow.

The event is described primarily in the Poetic Edda. In the poem Vafþrúðnismál, Odin poses the question to Vafþrúðnir as to who of mankind will survive the Fimbulwinter. Vafþrúðnir responds that Líf and Lífþrasir will survive and that they will live in the forest of Hoddmímis holt.

Ragnarök:

Ragnarök is a foretold series of impending events, including a great battle in which numerous great Norse mythological figures will perish (including the gods Odin, Thor, Týr, Freyr, Heimdall, and Loki); it will entail a catastrophic series of natural disasters, including the burning of the world, and culminate in the submersion of the world underwater. After these events, the world will rise again, cleansed and fertile, the surviving and returning gods will meet, and the world will be repopulated by two human survivors, Líf and Lífþrasir.

Chinese Mythology

Origin:

Chinese mythology originated around 1200 BCE during the Shang dynasty through oral traditions, regional folklore, and early records like oracle bones, later blending with Daoism, Confucianism, and Buddhism.

Introduction

Chinese mythology (traditional Chinese: 中國神話; simplified Chinese: 中国神话; pinyin: Zhōngguó shénhuà), is mythology that has been passed down in oral form or recorded in literature throughout the area now known as Greater China. Chinese mythology encompasses a diverse array of myths derived from regional and cultural traditions. Populated with engaging narratives featuring extraordinary individuals and beings endowed with magical powers, these stories often unfold in fantastical mythological realms or historical epochs. Similar to numerous other mythologies, Chinese mythology has historically been regarded, at least partially, as a factual record of the past.

Historical Events:

The Three Sovereigns Era:

From primal chaos emerged Pangu, who split the sky and earth. Following his death, divine god-kings like Fuxi and Nüwa taught humans to hunt, fish, and write, while Nüwa famously mended a catastrophic tear in the fabric of heaven using five-colored stones.

The Battle of Zhuolu:

A legendary, earth-shaking war where the Yellow Emperor fought the monstrous, copper-headed war god Chiyou for control of China. The conflict involved magical blinding fogs, the invention of the mechanical south-pointing chariot, and the fiery intervention of the Goddess of Drought.

The Great Flood of Gun-Yu (c. 2200 BC):

A massive, supernatural proxy war surrounding the real-world historical overthrow of the corrupt Shang Dynasty by the righteous Zhou Dynasty. The conflict saw rival factions of Daoist immortals, demons, and iconic deities like Nezha clash alongside mortal armies using mountain-crushing magical weapons.
